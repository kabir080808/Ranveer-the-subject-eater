# "The King Who Said Sorry in Stone" — original script v1 (2026-11-04)
Format: cartoon 3D story short (~65 s, ~3.7 words/s), one steady narrator voice, our own characters and story.

## Facts and sources
- Ashoka, third Mauryan emperor, ruled c. 268-232 BCE over most of the Indian subcontinent. (Wikipedia, "Ashoka"; "Maurya Empire")
- Kalinga war, c. 261 BCE, on the east coast (modern Odisha). Rock Edict XIII: "150,000 were deported, 100,000 were killed
  and many more died"; Ashoka records "deep remorse" for the conquest. (Major Rock Edict XIII, tr. S. Dhammika, "The Edicts of King Ashoka")
- Kalinga had war elephants (Megasthenes via Pliny: 700 elephants); the Mauryan army had thousands. (Wikipedia, "Kalinga War")
- Rock Edict I: "Formerly, in the kitchen of Beloved-of-the-Gods, King Piyadasi, hundreds of thousands of animals were killed
  every day to make curry. But now ... only three creatures, two peacocks and a deer are killed, and the deer not always.
  And in time, not even these three creatures will be killed." (tr. Dhammika)
- Edicts carved on rocks and polished sandstone pillars (some ~12-15 m tall) along roads and at towns, in local Prakrit
  (Brahmi and Kharosthi scripts), plus Greek and Aramaic in the north-west. (Wikipedia, "Edicts of Ashoka")
- Rock Edict II: medical treatment for humans and for animals, medicinal herbs planted. Pillar Edict VII: banyan shade trees,
  mango groves, wells every half-kos and rest houses along roads. (tr. Dhammika)
- The Lion Capital of Ashoka (Sarnath) is India's State Emblem, printed on rupee notes; the 24-spoke Ashoka Chakra is at the
  centre of the national flag. (Wikipedia, "Lion Capital of Ashoka"; "Flag of India")

## Script (~245 words, ~66 s)
The most powerful king in India won the biggest war of his life, and it made him so miserable he gave up war for good.
It's around 260 BC. Ashoka rules almost all of India, and just one kingdom still won't bow: Kalinga, on the east coast.
So he sends soldiers. Then war elephants. Then more soldiers.
Kalinga fights back for every field, and the battle just keeps getting bigger.
When it's finally over, Ashoka has won. A hundred thousand people are dead, and a hundred and fifty thousand more are marched away.
The conqueror looks at what he's won, and feels sick.
So he does something no emperor does. He says sorry. In writing. Carved into rock.
He even fixed the palace menu. The royal kitchen used to kill hundreds of thousands of animals a day for curry. Now it was down to two peacocks and a deer.
Luckily, that was a huge improvement.
Unluckily, if you were one of the peacocks.
But how do you tell millions of people anything, with no paper and no internet?
Think of the note on your fridge. Stick it where everyone walks past, and everyone reads it.
So Ashoka put his notes on giant stone pillars beside the busiest roads, in the local language.
Then he planted shade trees, dug wells, and set up medicine for people, and for animals.
Two thousand years later, his empire is gone. But his stone lions are on every rupee note, and his wheel sits in the middle of India's flag.
